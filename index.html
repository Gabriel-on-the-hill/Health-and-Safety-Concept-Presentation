⚠️ Why Do We Need Safety Rules?

Accidents are events caused by human errors and carelessness — they don't "just happen".

To avoid them, a set of regulations was introduced, first in 1988, called COSHH (Control of Substances Hazardous to Health). Workshops contain fast-spinning tools, sharp blades, toxic glues, and fragile glass — all of which can cause serious injury.

Following the workshop rules protects you, your classmates, your teacher, and even the cleaning staff.

WORKSHOP DISCIPLINE

The Five Golden Rules of Workshop Safety

1. Do NOT Run in the Workshop

NEVER run or engage in horseplay. Tripping near sharp chisels, saws, or powered machinery causes severe impact lacerations and bone fractures.

2. Wear Appropriate Personal Protective Equipment (PPE)

Always equip suitable PPE (goggles, lab coats, nose masks, earmuffs, gloves) matched precisely to the machinery or chemical operation.

3. Tidy Away & Clean Up All Resources After Use

Clean up sawdust, remove wood/glass offcuts, and return tools to storage racks so the workspace remains free from slip and trip hazards.

4. Know Emergency Equipment & Exit Locations

Locate first aid kits, emergency stop buttons (E-Stops), eye-wash stations, and emergency fire exits so you can act immediately in an emergency.

5. Ask Your Teacher for Guidance Whenever Unsure

If you are uncertain about setting up a machine, cutting glass, or using adhesives, ALWAYS ask your teacher or technician before proceeding.

UNDERSTANDING COSHH

COSHH — Control of Substances Hazardous to Health

🧪 What is COSHH?

  • What is it? COSHH is a set of UK regulations introduced in 1988 to protect people from dangerous substances at work and in school workshops.
  • In D&T: It covers substances like wood dust (especially MDF), glues and adhesives, solvent-based paints, soldering fumes, and varnishes.
  • Teacher's Duty: Provide ventilation (e.g. dust extractors, fume hoods), supply the correct PPE, and keep safety information available.
  • Your Duty: Work in well-ventilated areas, wear the PPE you are given, and report any spills or missing safety labels immediately.

⚠️ Common Workshop Hazardous Substances

Wood Fine Dust (MDF & Hardwood): Severe respiratory irritant and asthma trigger. Requires dust extraction & nose mask.
Frame Glues & Solvents: Toxic vapors causing dizziness & skin irritation. Requires well-ventilated space & nose mask.
Glass Sanding / Filing Dust: Microscopic glass splinters causing skin and lung irritation. Requires gloves & secure clamping.
COSHH SYMBOLS

The Nine COSHH Safety Symbols & Their Meanings

There are nine safety symbols associated with COSHH. Workshop Safety Symbols guide everyone in handling hazardous substances. (See the COSHH Symbols Worksheet for more activities)

EXPLOSIVE
Unstable chemicals prone to blast or fire shock.
FLAMMABLE
Catches fire easily (glues, solvent sprays, lacquers).
OXIDIZING
Releases oxygen; intensifies chemical fires.
GAS UNDER PRESSURE
Compressed gas cylinders (welding gas tanks).
CORROSIVE
Causes severe chemical skin burns and metal damage.
TOXIC / POISON
Fatal or toxic if swallowed, inhaled, or absorbed.
HARMFUL / IRRITANT
Causes skin irritation, allergies, or eye injury.
HEALTH HAZARD
Long-term respiratory asthma or organ damage.
ENVIRONMENTAL
Toxic to aquatic organisms & natural habitats.
SAFETY SIGNS IN THE WORKSHOP

Workshop Safety Sign Categories & Their Meanings

PROHIBITION

Red Circle + Slash

MUST NOT DO

Prohibits actions that increase risk. White background, black pictogram, red border and diagonal bar.

Example: "No Running", "No Food".

WARNING

Yellow Triangle

DANGER / CAUTION

Warns of specific hazards. Yellow background with black triangular border and black symbol.

Example: "Hot Surface", "Glass Hazard".

MANDATORY

Blue Circle

MUST DO ACTION

Mandatory safety instructions that MUST be followed before proceeding. Blue background, white icon.

Example: "Wear Goggles", "Wear Mask".

SAFE CONDITION

Green Rectangle

SAFETY / EMERGENCY

Indicates safe escape routes, first aid, or emergency equipment. Green background, white icon.

Example: "First Aid Kit", "Glass Waste Barrel".

PPE — PERSONAL PROTECTIVE EQUIPMENT

PPE: What to Wear & What It Protects

📋 As part of COSHH, PPE must be provided by the employer and worn by employees to protect them. Each item protects a specific part of the body.   (See the PPE Symbol Match-Up Worksheet for more activities)

Safety Goggles & Visors

PROTECTS: EYES & FACE

Protects eyes from flying wood/metal chips, glass splinters, and chemical splashes.

Mandatory: Drilling, sawing, glass cutting, and sanding.

Earmuffs & Earplugs

PROTECTS: HEARING

Protects eardrums from intense machine acoustic noise exceeding 85dB.

Mandatory: Routers, circular saws, thicknessers.

Nose Mask / Respirator

PROTECTS: RESPIRATORY SYSTEM

Prevents inhalation of toxic MDF dust, glass filing powder, and glue vapors.

Mandatory: Sanding, frame gluing, spraying.

Lab Coat / Overall

PROTECTS: BODY & CLOTHING

Protects skin/clothing from catching in rotating parts and chemical spills.

Note: Button up; roll loose sleeves above elbows.

Protective Hand Gloves

PROTECTS: HANDS (Specific Operations)

Protects hands when handling sharp glass offcuts, hot metals, or corrosive glues.

CRITICAL RULE: DO NOT wear gloves on drill press or lathe!

Steel Toe Boots / Shoes

PROTECTS: FEET & TOES

Protects feet from heavy dropped timber blocks, glass sheets, or heavy tools.

Mandatory: Closed solid footwear in workshop.

Safety Helmet

PROTECTS: HEAD

Protects against falling objects in heavy construction and industrial settings.

Note: Less common in school D&T but required on construction sites.

PRACTICAL CASE STUDY

Wooden Picture Frame & Glass Cutting Operation Analysis

Step 1: Cut Wood
Hazard: Hand injury while sawing timber frame joints.
Solution: Clamp wood securely in a vice or against bench hook. Keep non-cutting hand behind blade path.
Step 2: Glue & Clamp
Hazard: Toxic glue fumes; frame drying in walkway passage.
Solution: Work in well-ventilated space. Wear nose mask. Place frame in designated out-of-the-way drying zone.
Step 3: Cut Glass
Hazard: Cuts while cutting glass; unsafe glass offcut disposal.
Solution: Wear cut-resistant gloves. Dispose offcuts in clearly labelled glass waste barrels for cleaner safety.
Step 4: Smooth Edges
Hazard: Deep cuts while sandpapering or filing sharp raw glass.
Solution: Hold or clamp glass securely using padded vice. Wear protective gloves and safety goggles.
Step 5: Mount Glass
Hazard: Dropping or breaking glass sheet while lifting/fitting.
Solution: Ask a classmate to help lift (Teamwork). Discuss beforehand who will do what so actions align.
POWERED MACHINERY

Pillar Drill & Scroll Saw Operation Safeguards

⚙️ Pillar Drill (Drill Press)

Hazards: High-speed rotating chuck entangling loose hair/sleeves; flying swarf; unclamped workpiece spinning violently.

Safeguards & Solutions:

  • Lower clear chuck guard BEFORE switching on motor.
  • Securely clamp workpiece in machine vice.
  • Wear goggles; tie hair back; roll up loose sleeves.
  • REMOVE chuck key before turning on power!
  • NEVER wear gloves on the drill press!

🪚 Scroll Saw / Hegner Saw

Hazards: Reciprocating saw blade cutting fingers; blade snapping under stress; airborne timber dust.

Safeguards & Solutions:

  • Adjust hold-down foot guard so it rests on workpiece.
  • Use push stick for small timber components.
  • Keep fingers clear of direct blade cutting line.
  • Activate dust extraction system before cutting.
  • Wear safety goggles and dust mask.
RISK ASSESSMENT

Workshop Risk Assessment — Hazard, Risk & Control

HAZARD: Potential source of harm (e.g. sharp glass edge, toxic glue fumes, spinning drill).
RISK: Likelihood & severity of injury from hazard exposure.
CONTROL MEASURE: Action taken to eliminate/reduce risk to safe level.
Operation / Step Potential Hazard Initial Risk Control Measure (Solution) Residual Risk
1. Cut Wood with Saw Saw blade slip causing hand lacerations HIGH Clamp wood securely in vice; keep hands behind blade LOW
2. Frame Gluing & Drying Inhalation of toxic glue vapors MEDIUM Work in ventilated area, nose mask, safe drying zone LOW
3. Glass Cutting & Offcuts Cuts during glass cutting & waste cleanup HIGH Wear protective gloves, use labelled glass waste barrels LOW
4. Glass Edge Sanding Severe skin cuts during edge filing HIGH Clamp glass tight, wear cut-resistant gloves, file gently LOW
DESIGNER & CONSUMER SAFETY

The Designer's Responsibility & Safety Organizations

💡 Why Designers Must Think About Safety

  • Design out Hazards: Remove sharp corners, toxic finishes, and weak parts during the design stage — before the product is made.
  • Product Testing: Products are tested to make sure they meet the safety standards set by responsible organisations.
  • Safety Labels & Warnings: Products must be correctly labelled, with safety warnings put where necessary.
  • Keeping Customers Safe: The customer's safety is important — designers have a responsibility to make products safe to use.

🏢 Global Health & Safety Organizations

HSE (Health and Safety Executive): UK regulator enforcing workplace health, safety, & COSHH compliance.
BSI (British Standards Institution): Sets national quality standards & awards the famous "Kitemark" symbol.
OSHA (Occupational Safety & Health Admin): USA federal agency ensuring safe workplace conditions.
DATA (Design and Technology Association): Subject authority providing specialist D&T safety guidance for schools.
